**For an option specifically, the core pricing variables are:** **underlying price + strike + time to expiry + implied volatility + interest rate + dividends + option type**, with **Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega and Rho** measuring sensitivity to those variables.

**For a stock specifically, the biggest broad buckets are:** **earnings/fundamentals + valuation + expectations + interest rates/macro + industry/company news + market sentiment + supply/demand/flows + technical positioning**.

what are the most impacting and material out of these list, on which option/stock price daily depends on heavily.

## Assistant

The **daily price of a stock or option is not equally sensitive to all 350 factors**. In practice, a relatively small set explains most of the day-to-day movement. The exact importance changes by stock, market regime, and whether there is a company-specific event.

Here is the **material/core set**, roughly from the factors that most commonly drive daily price movement to the more secondary ones:

1. **Underlying stock price** — the primary driver of the option price  
2. **Supply and demand / order flow** — aggressive buyers vs sellers move price immediately  
3. **Market liquidity** — determines how much buying/selling is required to move price  
4. **News and new information** — company, sector, macro, geopolitical, regulatory, etc.  
5. **Market sentiment / risk-on-risk-off environment**  
6. **Implied volatility (IV)** — extremely important for options, especially around events  
7. **Time to expiry** — continuously affects option time value  
8. **Delta** — option sensitivity to movement in the underlying  
9. **Gamma** — how quickly Delta changes as the stock moves  
10. **Theta** — daily loss of option time value, especially near expiry  
11. **Vega** — option sensitivity to changes in implied volatility  
12. **Strike price / moneyness** — ITM, ATM, OTM materially changes option behavior  
13. **Open interest** — indicates where positions are concentrated and can affect flows  
14. **Change in open interest** — new positioning/unwinding can influence price and interpretation  
15. **Option volume / order flow** — actual trading activity in calls and puts  
16. **Put-call positioning / skew** — reveals relative demand for downside vs upside protection/speculation  
17. **Volatility skew / volatility surface** — different IVs across strikes and expiries  
18. **Earnings expectations** — expected revenue, EPS, margins and guidance  
19. **Actual earnings/results vs expectations** — often causes the largest single-day stock moves  
20. **Management guidance / forward outlook**  
21. **Interest rates / risk-free rate** — especially relevant for valuation and longer-dated options  
22. **Expected dividends** — materially affect stock price and option valuation  
23. **Benchmark/index movement** — Nifty/S&P 500/etc. can dominate individual stocks intraday  
24. **Sector movement** — banks, IT, energy, pharma, etc. can move together  
25. **Institutional buying/selling** — mutual funds, foreign institutions, pension funds, hedge funds, etc.  
26. **Futures positioning and futures price** — important for heavily traded stocks/indexes  
27. **Short interest / short covering** — can accelerate moves  
28. **Dealer/market-maker hedging flows** — particularly important in actively traded options  
29. **Gamma exposure / dealer gamma positioning** — can amplify or suppress intraday movement  
30. **Technical momentum / trend** — trend strength, breakouts, breakdowns and momentum flows  
31. **VWAP** — important intraday reference for institutional/algorithmic trading  
32. **Volume relative to normal volume** — unusual volume often accompanies important price discovery  
33. **Support and resistance levels** — can affect clustered orders and reactions  
34. **ATR / realized volatility** — indicates the stock's typical movement magnitude  
35. **Global market movement** — especially important for stocks with strong global correlations  
36. **Currency movement** — important for exporters/importers and multinational companies  
37. **Commodity prices** — critical for commodity-sensitive companies  
38. **Economic data releases** — inflation, GDP, employment, PMI, etc.  
39. **Central-bank decisions and rate expectations**  
40. **Corporate actions** — dividends, splits, buybacks, rights issues, dilution, M&A, etc.

### The most important distinction

For **stocks**, the daily hierarchy is usually centered around:

**News/catalyst → market/index → sector → order flow/liquidity → expectations/fundamentals → interest rates/macro → technical positioning**

For **options**, it is much more concentrated:

**Stock price → Delta/Gamma → Implied Volatility/Vega → Time to Expiry/Theta → Strike/Moneyness → Interest Rate → Dividends → option order flow/open interest → dealer hedging**

And there is an important practical point:

**Stock price movement drives Delta/Gamma effects, while IV changes can independently move the option price even when the stock barely moves.** Near expiry, **Gamma and Theta become particularly important**; around earnings or major events, **IV/Vega can become exceptionally important**.

A useful way to think about an option's intraday price is:

**Option Price ≈ f(Stock Price, Strike, Time, IV, Interest Rate, Dividend)**

and the **Greeks tell you how sensitive that option price is to changes in those variables**.

For active daily trading, the **smallest practical “must-watch” dashboard** is therefore:

**Underlying Price + Volume + Order Flow + VWAP + Open Interest + Change in OI + IV + IV Skew + Delta + Gamma + Theta + Vega + Time to Expiry + Strike/Moneyness + Index + Sector + News/Catalysts + Futures + Market Volatility + Interest Rates**

That is the core set I would consider **material enough to monitor continuously**, rather than trying to track all 350 factors equally.

**“How far can the underlying reasonably travel before the trade thesis encounters meaningful resistance, and what option premium corresponds to that move?”**

## Recommended architecture for SL / Entry / Target

**1. Direction Engine**  
Determines CE / PE / no-trade.

**2. Entry Engine**  
Determines *where* to enter.

**3. Stop Engine**  
Determines *where the thesis becomes invalid*.

**4. Target Engine**  
Determines *where the expected move becomes exhausted / reward becomes attractive*.

**5. Execution Engine**  
Checks spread, liquidity, slippage, order-book conditions and whether the calculated price is actually executable.

**6. Risk Engine**  
Checks position size, maximum loss, R:R, volatility regime and event risk.

The factors that tell you **“UP”** are not necessarily the factors that tell you **“BUY HERE,” “STOP HERE,” or “EXIT HERE.”**
